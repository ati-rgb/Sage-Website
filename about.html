Understanding Aging in Place Design

Creating beautiful, safe, functional homes that adapt to your changing needs while maintaining style and comfort for life.

What is Aging in Place?

The choice to remain in your own home safely, independently, and comfortably, regardless of age or ability level

A Growing Movement

Aging in place represents a fundamental shift in how we think about our homes and our futures. Rather than accepting that aging means moving to assisted living facilities or nursing homes, more Americans are choosing to modify their existing homes to support their changing needs.

This movement is driven by both emotional and practical considerations. Our homes contain our memories, our communities, and our sense of identity. They're where we feel most comfortable and connected. The financial implications are significant too - modifying an existing home is often far more cost-effective than moving to institutional care.

But aging in place isn't just about adding grab bars and ramps. It's about thoughtful design that anticipates and adapts to changing needs while maintaining the beauty, style, and personality that make a house feel like home.

Universal Design Principles

Creating spaces that work for everyone, regardless of age, size, or ability

Beyond Accessibility

Universal design goes beyond basic accessibility requirements to create spaces that are inherently usable by all people, to the greatest extent possible, without the need for adaptation or specialized design.

The beauty of universal design lies in its invisibility. When done well, these features enhance the experience for everyone while providing essential support for those who need it. A curbless shower isn't just accessible - it's elegant, spa-like, and easier to clean for everyone.

This approach represents a shift from reactive modifications (adding features when needed) to proactive design (incorporating universal principles from the start). The result is homes that are not only safer and more functional but also more valuable and marketable.

Room-by-Room Design Considerations

Practical applications of aging in place design throughout your home

Bathrooms

The highest-risk area for falls deserves special attention. Key considerations include:

  • Curbless, walk-in showers with built-in seating
  • Comfort-height toilets (17-19 inches)
  • Elegant grab bars that double as towel racks
  • Non-slip flooring with style
  • Excellent lighting and ventilation

Kitchens

The heart of the home should be both beautiful and functional for all users:

  • Varied counter heights for different tasks
  • Pull-out shelves and drawers
  • Lever-style handles and touchless faucets
  • Excellent task and ambient lighting
  • Clear pathways and turning space

Bedrooms

Creating a sanctuary that supports rest and safety:

  • Adjustable bed at optimal height (20-23 inches)
  • Clear pathways to bathroom
  • Motion-activated lighting
  • Accessible closet organization
  • Emergency communication systems

Living Areas

Comfortable gathering spaces that work for everyone:

  • Open floor plans with clear pathways
  • Stable, appropriately-sized furniture
  • Multiple lighting levels and sources
  • Non-slip, easy-maintenance flooring
  • Comfortable seating with good support
